Model subroutines

Devolatilization
  • Instantaneous release of volatiles
  • C/H/O elemental closure (no molecular H₂ closure)
  • Fixed carbon to the char inventory
Bubbling bed (two zones)
  • Hydrodynamics: Umf (Wen–Yu), bubble size (Mori–Wen) and fraction
  • Bubble–emulsion gas interchange (Kunii–Levenspiel)
  • Emulsion: volatiles, char and catalytic tar reactions
  • Bubbles: homogeneous oxidation
Freeboard (plug flow)
  • Isothermal PFR, homogeneous kinetics
  • Kinetic, reversible water–gas shift
Reaction network
  • 26 reactions: 19 homogeneous, 3 char, 4 on dolomite
  • Each traced to its primary source; gas kinetics not fitted
  • Dolomite: apparent kinetics (pore diffusion), activity calibrated on the reference-case tar

About the model

One-dimensional kinetic model of air gasification in a bubbling fluidized bed: two-zone bed (bubble and emulsion phases, Kunii–Levenspiel interchange), plug-flow freeboard and a 26-reaction network traced to primary sources. The gas-phase kinetics are not fitted; only the activity of the in-bed dolomite is calibrated on the tar yields of the reference case. The plant is sized for the heat demand, and the economics (NPV, IRR, LCOH) and GWP100 follow from the predicted syngas. The Python code runs in your browser.

Use with care: the bed temperature is imposed, tar yields are calibrated only for dolomite beds of the reference case and are indicative elsewhere, and results far from the conditions of the validation (700–1100 °C, ER 0.2–0.4) are extrapolations. Source: Chuquín-Vasco, D., Lo-Iacono-Ferreira, V. G., Terrados-Cristos, M., Carmo-Calado, L., Torregrosa-López, J. I. Open-source kinetic modeling of olive pomace–RDF co-gasification in a bubbling fluidized bed: validation, techno-economic and carbon-footprint assessment.

How to use the lab

  1. Wait until the engine indicator turns green (“Model ready”). The first load downloads Python, NumPy and SciPy (~30 MB) and is cached afterwards.
  2. Choose a preset or set the inputs in panel 1. In the fuel table, the ultimate analysis is on a dry basis (wt%) and the proximate analysis as received (wt%).
  3. Press Run model. Outputs appear in panel 4; warnings flag inputs outside the validated window.
  4. Press Record run to add it to the data table (panel 6), which you can copy as CSV or print.
  5. Use panel 5 to sweep temperature, ER or RDF fraction.

ER is defined on the elemental basis: ER = air / (stoichiometric air × feed rate). Gas volumes at 0 °C and 1 atm. Dry composition is tar-free and includes N₂.
